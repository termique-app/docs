A snippet is a saved command or script. Run it in the focused session from the snippet palette, or run it automatically when you connect to a host. Use snippets for deploys, service restarts, status checks, or anything you type more than once.

Open **Snippets** under **Tools** in the sidebar.

## Create a snippet

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open New snippet">
    Click **New snippet** in the Snippets header.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the command">
    Type or paste the **Command**, e.g. `sudo systemctl restart nginx`. Multi-line commands are supported and are sent exactly as typed. Click **Next**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the details">
    Enter a **Label** (e.g. `Restart nginx`), an optional **Description**, and up to 5 **Tags** (press `Enter` after each). Turn on **Share publicly** to list it in the [Marketplace](#marketplace).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save snippet**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Free accounts are limited to 10 snippets. Forking a Marketplace snippet counts toward this limit. Upgrade to [Pro](/plans) for unlimited snippets.
</Note>

## Run a snippet

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the snippet palette">
    In a session, press `⌘⇧K` (macOS) or `Ctrl+Shift+K` (Windows and Linux), or click **Run a snippet** in the session header.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a snippet">
    Type to search by label, command, or description. Press `Enter` or click the snippet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Termique shows the full command. Click **Run on `<host>`** to send it to the focused pane, followed by `Enter`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The palette runs a snippet only in the focused pane, and only while that session is connected. Without one, the palette copies the command to your clipboard instead. Press `⌘C` or `Ctrl+C` in the palette to copy the highlighted snippet without running it.

## Run a snippet on connect

On a host's detail page, choose a snippet under **Startup snippet**. Termique runs it as soon as a session to that host connects. See [Startup snippet](/hosts#startup-snippet).

## Manage your snippets

**My snippets** lists your snippets with their command, tags, scope (**Public** or **Private**), and when they were last updated.

- Type in **Filter snippets** to search labels, commands, descriptions, and tags.
- Click **Display** to show **All**, **Public**, or **Private** snippets, and to order by **Recently updated** or **Name**.
- Hover a row and click **Copy command** to copy it.
- Double-click a row, or press `Enter` on it, to edit it. You can also open **More actions** (`…`) and select **Edit**.
- To delete a snippet, open **More actions** and select **Delete**, then confirm.

## Marketplace

The **Marketplace** tab lists public snippets from all Termique users.

- Type in **Search public snippets** to search.
- Use **Sort** to order by **Most forked**, **Newest**, or **Best match**.
- Use **Tag** to filter by one or more tags.

Click a snippet, or its **Fork** button, to review it. The panel shows the author, fork count, description, tags, and the full command. Invisible characters, such as zero-width or text-direction characters, are shown as markers like `⟨U+202E⟩` so the command you read is the command that runs.

Click **Fork to my snippets** to save a private copy you can edit and run. Click **Report snippet** to flag it as **Destructive**, **Malicious**, **Spam**, or **Other**.

<Warning>
  Read a community command in full before you run it, especially on a production host. If you attach a forked snippet as a host's startup snippet, Termique asks you to confirm because it will run unattended on every connect.
</Warning>

### Publish a snippet

Turn on **Share publicly** when you create or edit a snippet. Anyone can then find and fork it. Do not publish a snippet that contains a hostname, credential, or other sensitive value.

A public snippet that receives reports can be hidden or removed from the Marketplace. It then shows a **hidden** or **removed** badge in **My snippets**. You can still use it yourself.

## Sync

Snippets sync through the API to your other devices. They are not end-to-end encrypted. Do not store credentials or sensitive values in snippet commands.
